Spider-Man Sighting Analytics

Original pitch — unchanged

"Where is Most Likely to Meet Spider-Man?" — Spider-Man treated as a high-value emergency responder subject to spatial/infrastructure constraints. A Spider-Man Sighting Probability Index (SSPI) ranks NYC neighborhoods:

SSPI = 0.40 (Building Density) + 0.40 (Crime Density) + 0.20 (Traffic Index)
*normalized on a 0-1 min-max scale before linear weighting

The three decision variables as originally framed: (1) Vertical Density (40%) — web-pendulum mechanics need tall anchors ≥100m; outer-borough low-rises limit movement speed. (2) Operational Demand (40%) — NYPD precinct data on violent crime, robberies, high-priority calls. (3) Traffic Advantage (20%) — gridlock gives web-swinging a speed edge over ground units.

Open gaps to say out loud
  • Crime data is 11–13 years old — fine for a relative ranking, not for "today's crime rate." The live full dataset (10M rows, 2006–present) exists for a v2 pull.
  • Traffic is a substitute, not the real thing — crash density, not congestion/speed. State as a scope compromise.
  • Neighborhood boundaries are approximate — DUMBO's NTA really absorbs Downtown Brooklyn (likely inflates its score), and UWS/UES/Astoria/Chelsea each required combining multiple sub-NTAs.

NYC Restroom Gap & ROI

Original pitch — unchanged

"When Nature Calls, Where Should NYC Answer?" — NYC Council reported 973 operational public restrooms, only 8 open 24/7, 49.42% of residents within a 5-minute walk (Nov 2025). The project asks where access is weakest around tourist attractions, subway stations, and parks, and whether the city should extend hours, reopen, deploy modular units, or build permanently.

Primary audience: Mayor's Office of Operations (set citywide priorities), NYC Parks (reopen/repair/extend/build), NYC DOT Public Realm (site modular units), MTA (flag high-ridership stations with weak nearby access).

Two-stage model: Stage 1—Need ranks 500m hexagons or neighborhood units; Stage 2—Intervention compares alternatives only within the verified high-need shortlist, so a high need score isn't mistaken for a high-return project.

RGI = 100 x [0.35 Demand + 0.30 Supply Gap + 0.15 Hours Gap + 0.10 Accessibility Gap + 0.10 Equity Need]
Demand = 0.35 Subway Ridership + 0.25 Taxi Activity + 0.20 Tourist POIs + 0.20 Park Exposure

Social ROI: SROI = NPV ÷ CapEx, also cost per added visit/open hour/person within 5-min walk. NYCEDC benchmark: $4M for 17 modular units (production, delivery, installation, maintenance) — not a clean construction price, use ranges. Stated limitations (already in the original deck): restroom status isn't real-time; POI count ≠ visitation; park acreage ≠ attendance; MTA OD ridership is estimated; taxi activity omits walkers/bus riders.
